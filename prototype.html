The Fifth Card & the Coin

Fifty-four cards, both jokers in. A spectator picks five and flips a coin. The assistant lays four cards face up in a row and says nothing. The magician, out of the room throughout, names the fifth card and calls the coin.

Five steps each side. Pick a role below and you only ever see your own.

Magician

You see four cards in a row. You name the fifth and the coin.

  1. Find the lowest card in the row. Slots 1 and 2 are heads, slots 3 and 4 are tails. Slots 1 and 3 mean m is under six, slots 2 and 4 mean add six.
  2. The other three, left to right, give m mod 6: LMH 0 · LHM 1 · MLH 2 · MHL 3 · HLM 4 · HML 5. Add to get m.
  3. Add the four numbers. start = 5 minus the remainder mod 5, and a remainder of 0 means start is 5.
  4. u = 5m + start.
  5. Write the four shown numbers in order and subtract 0, 1, 2, 3 from them. Count how many of those four u reaches or passes, and add that to u. That number is the hidden card.

Both jokers must be tellable apart. If yours are identical, put a pencil dot on one and agree which is which before you ever perform.

Where the numbers come from

You never need this to perform, but it makes the five steps stop feeling arbitrary.

Lay the deck out in order, 1 to 54, and take out the four cards on the table. Fifty are left. Push them together and renumber them 1 to 50. Every fifth one of those survivors is a candidate, and there are exactly ten because fifty divides by five. The assistant is telling the magician which of the ten, and that number is m.

The assistant's subtraction is just a shortcut for finding the hidden card's place among the survivors. Exactly i of the four shown cards sit below it, since it is the card i up from the bottom of the five, so its place is its value minus i. The magician's checkpoints do the same conversion backwards, since subtracting 0, 1, 2 and 3 from the shown cards marks the points where a survivor number and a card number pull another step apart.

The encoding itself is a counting fact. Four cards shown in order out of five carry 5 × 4! = 120 signals: 24 from the arrangement and a factor of five from choosing which card to withhold. Naming one card out of fifty costs 100, so the coin fits in the slack and a three-way choice never could.

How to rehearse

Pick your side and stay on it. You will only ever perform one of them, and learning both at once is how pairs stall.

  • Card values first, both of you. Until you stop computing and start recognising, about a card a second. Everything above this is gated on it and most pairs quit the drill too early.
  • Then your own middle two drills until the arithmetic is boring rather than effortful.
  • Then the full drill for your side, against the clock.
  • Then together, with real cards and a real coin, and the magician actually out of the room. Use Check a hand to settle arguments.

On the floor

  • The assistant needs half a minute or so. Buy it with business: squaring the deck, having the spectator initial the coin, asking them to close a fist over it. Silence and a fixed stare is the tell.
  • Slot position is the entire coin. Lay the row down deliberately and never nudge a card afterwards to straighten the line.
  • Call the card first, then the coin. The card is the trick; the coin is what they repeat to other people.
  • If the magician names a card that is face up on the table, something went wrong upstream. Agree in advance on how to die gracefully.
